Gold can go up for a week, fall for two days and then jump again. These moves look random, but they usually come from a handful of causes. Knowing them won't let anyone predict tomorrow's price, but it does make the daily news much easier to follow.

The US dollar

Gold is priced in dollars around the world. When the dollar becomes stronger against other currencies, gold becomes costlier for buyers outside the US, demand drops, and the price in dollars often falls. A weaker dollar usually has the opposite effect.

Interest rates

Gold does not pay interest. When banks offer high interest on deposits and bonds, some investors move money out of gold to earn that interest. When interest rates are expected to come down, gold often becomes more attractive again. This is why news about the US central bank (the Federal Reserve) often moves gold prices.

Fear and uncertainty

Gold has been treated as a "safe" store of value for thousands of years. During wars, financial crises or big political shocks, many people and institutions buy gold, and the price tends to rise. When calm returns, some of that buying stops.

Central banks

National central banks, including the Reserve Bank of India, keep part of their reserves in gold. When several central banks buy large amounts in the same period, it supports the price over months, not just days.

The rupee

For Indian buyers there is an extra factor: the rupee. Even if the world price stays flat, a weaker rupee makes gold more expensive in India. Read more in how the gold rate in India is decided.

Indian demand and seasons

India is one of the largest buyers of gold in the world. Demand rises during:

  • The wedding season, which in many regions runs from about October to February, with a second peak in April–May.
  • Festivals such as Akshaya Tritiya, Dhanteras and Diwali, when buying gold is considered lucky.
  • Good harvests, because many rural families save in gold after selling their crops.

Strong local demand mostly shows up as a higher premium at dealers rather than a change in the world price.

Government decisions

Changes to import duty, rules on how much gold can be imported, or new savings products from the government can move the Indian price on the day they are announced.

What this means for you

  • Moves of around 1% in a day are common, and bigger jumps happen on days with major news or a duty change.

Nobody can reliably predict short-term gold prices. This page explains the causes; it is not a recommendation to buy or sell.

A common myth: "gold always goes up"

Over many decades gold has risen a lot in rupee terms, partly because the rupee has weakened against the dollar. But it does not rise every year. After a peak in 2011, for example, the world gold price fell and stayed lower for several years. Anyone buying gold should be ready for periods when the price falls or stays flat.
